Falling Sky - a poem by Ernest R. Shifflett

Falling Sky

Last week the sky fell on me. A dark cloud wrapped its blanket All over me, tightened, penetrated pores, Numbing organs, seizing my heart. This phenomenon started with a phone call From my son revealing the dreaded C word, Out of the blue, weighing mountains of fear. For days I did not function, just Barely managed to breathe, keep living. Then something shattered my cloud shroud, Not destroying it, breaking it into pieces, Still there, always nagging, but bearable, Barely. For a control person to lose control, No redeeming action available, To be nakedly vulnerable Is a total change, demanding changes In perspective, expectations, hopes. Now only to wait and Hope. Oct. 21, 2006: Email to his Dad from Karl after reading this poem. “Wow. No penned words can say. Off the chart, needle broken. I think we all felt or feel this way. With an Ecclesiastical conclusion of prayer, wait and hope; there can only be one outcome. Matthew 8:17. This will be treasured by me forever. Love, Karl.”
