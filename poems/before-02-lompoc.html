Lompoc

Summer nights are cool, Days wake up drowsily, Brushing the morning fog away– Slowly by letting the hazy sun through— Followed by brisk ocean breezes, Some would say winds. Weekends, those breezes, that wind, Waft enticing aromas of BBQ’s, Grilling away for some group or charity, Tantalizing all our taste senses. When I follow one of many bike lanes Out of town, I see masses of flowers, Wind waving the pastel colors of sweet peas, Brilliant oranges, bright yellows. When I bicycle into town I see colorful paintings on numerous buildings, Murals dressing up previously drab walls, Pleasing the eye and promising hope. We don’t have the town hall meetings, We do have regular group gatherings— The farmers market, flea market, Old town festivals, where we meet, Talk, buy and sell, look and listen. Maybe, best of all, We have no parking meters.
